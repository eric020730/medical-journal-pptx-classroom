# Slide Structure Templates

Worked outlines for different paper types. Use as a starting skeleton; adjust
to fit the actual paper.

## Review article / "How I do it" (~6-10 pages, 5-8 figures)

Target: 45-50 slides. This is the pattern the reference deck follows.

```
 1. Title
 2. Outline
 3. Part 1 — Background
 4. Background: the disease / problem burden
 5. Background: historical evidence / screening landscape
 6. Background: current challenges
 7. Part 2 — Terminology & Definitions
 8. Core definition
 9. Classification evolution
10. Figure 1 (if paper shows a classification figure here)
11. Inclusion / exclusion criteria
12. Part 3 — Evidence: Study Set A
13-15. Cited study #1 (study design, cohort, results)
16. Figure from study #1 (if any)
17-18. Cited study #2
19-20. Cited study #3
21. Table summarizing Studies A (maps to Table 1 or 2)
22. Reproducibility / interreader discussion
23. Part 4 — Evidence: Study Set B
24-26. Cited studies / figures
27. Part 5 — Further evidence or sub-topic
28-29. ...
30. Part 6 — Guidelines / standards update if relevant
31. Guideline comparison
32. Clinical impact
33. Part 7 — Prediction / diagnostics
34-36. Predictor signs / features + figures
37. Figure: sign A
38. Figure: sign B
39. Figure: sign C
40. Part 8 — Clinical Algorithm
41. Algorithm: benign pathway
42. Algorithm: red flags / escalation pathway
43. Part 9 — Conclusions
44. Key Takeaways
45. Clinical Recommendations (by audience)
46. Key References
47. Thank You
```

Rule of thumb: every major cited study → 1-2 slides. Every figure/table in
the paper → 1 slide. Every distinct concept that needs explaining → 1 slide.

## How to decide

Once you've finished reading the paper, sketch:
- How many major sections does the paper have? → `Part N` count.
- How many figures + tables? → figure slides count.
- How many distinct cited studies anchor the argument? → evidence slide count.

Also sketch the title map before writing slide bodies:

- List the paper's native major headings in order.
- Under each major heading, list native subheadings in order.
- Assign each planned slide to one heading/subheading.
- Use those heading/subheading names as the slide-title backbone. If a section
  needs multiple slides, keep the heading wording stable and add a brief
  qualifier after a colon.

Sum these plus ~8 fixed slides (Title, Outline, Background context, Key
Takeaways, Recommendations, References, Thank You, and some buffer). If the
total is under 40, flesh out Background, Definitions, and Discussion. If it's
over 55, merge related evidence studies or drop minor figures.
