# Flexible Teaching-Deck Structure

Use the article's evidence, headings, figures, and tables to plan a complete
40–55-slide presentation. These topic groups are planning aids, not a fixed
sequence, specialty template, figure-number assignment, or requirement to
invent missing evidence.

## Shared presentation elements

- Title: the actual article title, author list, journal, and publication data.
- Outline: the real teaching sections with accurate slide ranges.
- Background: the clinical question, context, uncertainty, and study purpose.
- Section dividers: major topic changes, each with Traditional Chinese notes.
- Methods and evidence: design, population, measurements, analysis, bias, and
  uncertainty appropriate to the article type.
- Results: reported findings, effect sizes, confidence intervals, figures,
  tables, and relevant subgroup or sensitivity analyses.
- Interpretation: applicability, limitations, clinical meaning, and remaining
  research questions.
- Closing: evidence-based takeaways, recommendations when justified,
  references, and a concise thank-you slide.

## Adapt to the actual article

For original research, allocate more slides to study design, population,
endpoints, statistical methods, primary findings, secondary findings, and
limitations. Introduce each source figure or table where the surrounding
article text explains its role.

For narrative reviews, organize the deck around the paper's own concepts,
evidence groups, definitions, comparisons, practical implications, and stated
limitations. Distinguish cited evidence from the review authors' interpretation.

For systematic reviews and meta-analyses, cover the research question,
eligibility criteria, search process, study selection, bias assessment, pooled
effects, heterogeneity, sensitivity analysis, certainty of evidence, and
clinical applicability whenever those elements exist in the source.

For clinical reports or series, discuss the reported presentation, diagnostic
reasoning, intervention, follow-up, relevant evidence, uncertainty, and
generalizable learning points without inventing clinical details or presenting
an individual observation as broader proof.

For methodology or guideline articles, follow the authors' actual framework,
definitions, decision pathways, evaluation criteria, recommendations, and
limitations. Distinguish evidence-based guidance from expert opinion.

## Size, titles, and source visuals

Count mandatory structural slides, major source headings, substantive teaching
points, figures, and tables before writing. Expand legitimate background,
methods, interpretation, or limitations when the initial plan has fewer than
40 slides; combine closely related discussion points when it exceeds 55.

Map every planned slide to an actual source heading or subheading. Preserve the
heading wording where possible and use a short qualifier only when several
slides share the same heading.

Each source figure becomes exactly one recomposed figure slide regardless of
its panel count. A large source table may span multiple slides only when every
part retains its headers, complete content, safety margins, and consistent
on-screen display width. Record any intentionally omitted figure or table.
